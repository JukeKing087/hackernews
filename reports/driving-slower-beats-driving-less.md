Two of them cost nothing: drive slower or drive less. A household that drives 5% fewer miles has ~5% fewer chances to crash, assuming the miles cut are as dangerous as the miles kept. Rune Elvik’s 2009 meta-analysis of 115 studies found that fatal crashes fall much faster than speed does. For example, going from 65 to 60 mph on a freeway (an 8% cut in speed) cuts fatal crashes ~28%. Going from 35 to 30 mph (a 14% cut) cuts them ~33%. To frame that in time costs, it costs ~90 seconds to drive 60 vs. 65mph on a 20-mile freeway trip.
